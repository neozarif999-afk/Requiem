    The seven-step research method

    1. Read the background guide twice.First read for vocabulary; second for the chair's framing. Note every loaded word.
    2. Locate your country in the issue.Find at least one official statement, voting record, or policy paper from your country on this exact topic.
    3. Map allies and adversaries.Identify three or four aligned states and two or three that would oppose any draft you sponsor.
    4. Find the relevant resolutions.Look up the most recent GA, SC, or ECOSOC resolutions on the topic. Use operative clauses as a template.
    5. Collect three quantitative facts.Numbers from World Bank, SIPRI, OECD, or UNDP. One number is worth ten adjectives at the dais.
    6. Draft your policy in five clauses.Action, by whom, on what timeline, with what funding, and what review mechanism.
    7. Write a one-page brief.Position, three reasons, five facts, two solutions, three allies. Carry it. It is the only paper that matters.

      How to deploy a Point of Information

      A POI is a question directed to a speaker during their yielded time. It must be a genuine question — not a statement in disguise. The chair decides whether to allow it.

      The Treaty Gap

      "Would the delegate clarify whether [country] has ratified [treaty], and if not, how it justifies the position just stated?"

      The Voting Record

      "The delegate advocates for [X]. Would they explain their delegation's vote against GA resolution [number], which called for exactly that?"

      The Data Challenge

      "Would the delegate reconcile their claim with [source]'s [year] data, which shows [contradictory figure]?"

      Rules of Engagement

      Rise, state "Point of Information." Wait for recognition. Ask one targeted question. Sit. Never turn a POI into a speech — the chair will cut you off.
